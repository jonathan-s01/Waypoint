TurnOver: the guide

HOW TO USE WAYPOINT

Waypoint is a screen reader for the Mac: a second choice beside VoiceOver, not an add-on to it. While Waypoint is on,
it reads everything, with its own voice and the keys you already know, password fields and macOS's own prompts
included, and VoiceOver is off. Turn Waypoint off and VoiceOver comes back. If you'd rather VoiceOver kept some apps,
it can: see Keeping some apps with VoiceOver, below.
This page is the short tour. Each section links to the guide that goes into detail.

Three things to remember

1. Command Control Comma is the way in to everything Waypoint: press it, hear "Waypoint", then a letter. M is the
   Waypoint menu, S the Settings window, K the keyboard style, H these guides. Any other key reads the list.
   Command Control M opens the menu in one press. The menu is short: This app, Settings, Keys, Sounds, Tools,
   Help, Support Waypoint, Quit. Every setting lives in the Settings window, in sections down the side.
2. Command Control Period turns Waypoint off and on, from anywhere. Off, VoiceOver reads as usual.
3. If Waypoint ever stops responding, VoiceOver comes on within a few seconds and Waypoint starts again.

Everything else is the keys of the screen reader you already know. See The Waypoint layer.

Keeping some apps with VoiceOver

If VoiceOver reads an app better for you, it can keep that app while Waypoint reads the rest. In the app, press
Command Control Shift T: VoiceOver takes it over, and Waypoint remembers your choice. The same key gives it back
to Waypoint.

The first press turns on Readers per app (Settings, Screen reader behaviour). The list of VoiceOver's apps is
under Tools in the Waypoint menu. More in Waypoint and VoiceOver.

Choose your keys

Waypoint speaks three screen readers' keys. Press Command Control Comma, then K, until you hear the one you want:

- VoiceOver keys (the Mac style). Every key. VO is Control and Option together (or Caps
  Lock); VO arrows move, VO Space presses, VO Shift Down goes into things, VO U is the rotor.
- NVDA keys (the Windows style). Every key. Caps Lock is the NVDA key; Caps Lock L reads
  the line, Caps Lock A reads to the end, Caps Lock F7 lists the links and headings, H and K jump by heading and link
  on a web page.
- JAWS keys. Every key. JAWS's laptop layout: Caps Lock U, I and O for lines, J, K and L for
  words, Caps Lock Z for the virtual cursor, Caps Lock F6 and F7 for the lists.

The style changes which keys you press, never what Waypoint can do. Keyboard styles explains the
differences and how the three fit on a Mac keyboard.

Reading

Arrow through text, documents, web pages and messages; say all reads to the end and stops where you were. In a web
page or a message, single letters jump by heading, link, button or form field, and Return or Space presses. See
Reading and say all.

The apps

Waypoint reads every app. For the ones people use most, it adds keys and reading of its own: Mail, Messages and
WhatsApp, Word and Excel, Safari, Music, Podcasts, Calendar, Pages, Numbers, Keynote, Terminal, Steam and more.
Each app's own keys are listed at the end of the key pages, and described in The apps. An app that
VoiceOver does better can stay VoiceOver's: Command Control Shift T.

When something isn't read well

- Screen recognition (Command Control Comma, then R) reads a window from the screen itself, for apps that tell the
  Mac nothing about their windows.
- Plugins teach Waypoint an app: its names for things, its keys, what to read. Anyone can write one, as data, no
  programming needed, and share it. See Plugins.
- Give the app to VoiceOver with Command Control Shift T.

Help and problems

- Keyboard help: the Waypoint menu, Keys, then keyboard help: every key says what it does without doing it.
- Every shortcut here: Command Control Comma, then Slash: the keys that work where you are, in a list.
- Something wrong: Troubleshooting and feedback. Reports go through GitHub,
  where everyone can see and help with them.
- What Waypoint does with your data: Privacy and security.

Waypoint is a fork of TurnOver, free and open source, made in spare time. The code, the plugins and the ways to help are at
github.com/TurnOver-For-Mac/turnover.


GETTING STARTED

Waypoint is a screen reader for the Mac. It uses VoiceOver's keys, so what you already know works, and it reads
the apps you use every day more simply than VoiceOver does. It runs on macOS 14 or later, on Apple silicon and Intel.

Installing

1. Move Waypoint to your Applications folder and open it. A welcome message explains what it needs.
2. Turn Waypoint on in System Settings, Privacy and Security, under Accessibility (called Device Control and
   Data Access on macOS 27) and under Input Monitoring. macOS asks for the first; if it doesn't ask for the
   second, Waypoint tells you where it is.

Starting and stopping

- Command Control Period starts Waypoint, or stops it, from anywhere. A rising chime means on, a falling one off.
  While Waypoint is on it reads everything, password fields and macOS's own prompts included, and VoiceOver is off.
  When Waypoint stops, VoiceOver comes back.
- Right Option, Right Shift and slash turns Waypoint off or on without starting VoiceOver. Off, nothing reads, and it
  stays off after a restart. Nothing speaks at the lock screen or in password prompts while it is off, so turn it on
  first, or use Command Control Period to hand the Mac to VoiceOver.
- Command Control Shift T keeps the app in front with VoiceOver, or gives it back to Waypoint, and remembers it.
- To start Waypoint when you log in: the Waypoint menu, Settings, Starting, Open Waypoint at login.

The first five minutes

- Press Command Control Comma. Waypoint says "Waypoint": press M for the Waypoint menu. Up and Down move,
  Right or Return opens, Escape closes.
- Press Command Control Comma twice to hear every letter you can press after it.
- Your VoiceOver voice, speed and verbosity can come with you: the Waypoint menu, Settings, Copy VoiceOver settings, then Copy. Revert, beside it, puts back what you had before.

Updates

Waypoint checks once a day. When there is a new build it says so and asks whether to install it. The Waypoint menu,
About and updates, Updates checks now; What's new lists every build's changes.


THE WAYPOINT LAYER

Waypoint keeps its own commands behind one key, so it never takes a shortcut away from your apps: press
Command Control Comma, hear "Waypoint", then press a letter. It works in every app, including ones VoiceOver reads.

The letters

  Letter: What it does
  M: The Waypoint menu
  S: The Settings window: every setting, sections down the side
  T: Switches the app in front between Waypoint and VoiceOver, and remembers it (also straight away: Command Control Shift T)
  D: Direct: every key goes straight to the app in front and neither Waypoint nor VoiceOver says anything there, for a game or app that speaks for itself. Command shortcuts (Command Tab, Command Q) still work. D again to leave; remembered
  A: Who reads: Waypoint in its apps, Waypoint everywhere, or VoiceOver everywhere
  V: Copies your VoiceOver settings: voice, speed, pitch, verbosity
  K: Goes round the keyboard styles: VoiceOver keys, NVDA keys, JAWS keys
  J: "That was wrong": marks this moment for your next feedback report
  H: This guide
  I: Describes the picture under Waypoint's cursor
  C: Copies the last thing Waypoint said
  G: Trackpad gesture control on or off (experimental)
  R: Screen recognition: reads a window from the screen itself
  Slash: Every shortcut that works where you are, in a list; Return runs one

Any other key says the list again. Escape, or three seconds with no letter, cancels.

Command Control Period

A single press starts or stops Waypoint from anywhere, even if Waypoint has stopped answering: a small helper,
Waypoint Key, listens for it from login.

The Waypoint menu

- This app: who reads the app in front, and that app's own commands. Apps whose support is new say
  "experimental support".
- Settings: speech (with the say all voice), keyboard, braille, documents, web pages, sounds, and Experimental.
- Apps: which apps Waypoint reads.
- Keys: every shortcut, keyboard help, and your own keys.
- Send feedback, About and updates, Quit.

In the menu, Right or Return opens a submenu or changes a setting; on a number (rate, volume) Left turns it down.
Settings that restart Waypoint change only with Return.


READING AND SAY ALL

Web pages and messages

Web pages and email messages are read as a document. Up and Down go line by line; Option with the arrows, word by
word. Single letters jump: in the Mac style, VoiceOver's Quick Nav letters (H heading, L link, F text field, T table,
1 to 6 headings by level); in the Windows style, NVDA's. Waypoint Space switches between reading the page and
typing into it. Enter activates a link or button.

Which letters (Waypoint): Settings, Web pages, "Single-letter keys on a page" chooses VoiceOver's letters, NVDA's
(K link, L list, D landmark, I list item, B button, F form field…) or whichever the keyboard style uses; any keyboard
style can use either. To give one kind your own letter, add it to `virtualBuffers.quickNavKeys` in the settings file,
by kind name, for example `"quickNavKeys": {"Landmark": "y"}`; that kind's usual letter then stops working. Shift
goes back. In a field you are typing into, letters are typed, not commands.

Say all

Say all reads from where you are to the end: VO A in the Mac style, Caps Lock A in the Windows
style (Caps Lock Down Arrow with the desktop keyboard layout). It moves the caret as it goes.

- Any key stops it, and the caret is left on the word you had reached, not the start of the paragraph.
- Up and Down during say all skim: they move a line and carry on reading from there.

The say all voice

Say all can read in a natural voice made on your Mac, while everything else (moving, typing, menus) stays in your
usual voice, which answers at once. The Waypoint menu, Settings, Speech, Say all voice: "Same as speech", or a
natural voice. Natural voice speed is there too.

The natural voice reads ahead: while one paragraph plays, the next ones are being made, so it never waits.
Natural voices speak English for now; other languages keep your Mac's voices.

Documents

In Word, Pages and TextEdit, the arrows read as they move. VO Command H goes to the next heading (Shift for the
previous). In Pages, a table's line says its size; VO Shift Down reads it cell by cell (the arrows move, each
cell said with its row or column heading), and VO Shift Up or Escape comes back. Comments and highlights are
said after the line that holds them. Waypoint says the page as you move onto it.

PDFs in Preview (experimental)

A PDF is read like a web page: line by line across pages, with "page 2 of 5" as you reach each page, and headings
by H. Highlights, notes and text boxes, which Preview hides from VoiceOver, are read where they sit.


THE APPS

What Waypoint adds in each app it reads. To give an app to Waypoint or back to VoiceOver: Command Control Comma, T.

Mail

Up and Down move through the messages; Home and End go to the first and last. The message is read as a document.
Tab goes from the list to the message, Shift Tab to the mailboxes. Command Shift D sends, and Waypoint says "sent".

Messages and WhatsApp

The keyboard stays in the message box. Shift Tab goes up to the messages, newest first; Up and Down move; the
shortcut menu (VO Shift M) lists a message's actions; Escape goes back to the box. Command Control 1 to 9 read the
latest messages from anywhere, 1 the newest. New messages are read as they arrive.

Music, Podcasts and TV

Up and Down move through the page, shelf by shelf; Left and Right jump between shelves; Return plays; Space pauses
and says which. In Music and Podcasts, Return in the search field gives the results as a plain list from Apple's
catalog, as in the App Store; TV reads its own results page.

The App Store

Up and Down move through every app and button; Left and Right between shelves. Return opens an app's page; Space
presses its own button (Get, Open, Update). Command F searches: type, press Return, and the results are a plain list
from Apple's catalog while the search field keeps the focus. Up and Down move through it, Return opens one, Escape
leaves the list.

Microsoft Word and Excel

Word reads as you move, says the page as you reach it, and has VoiceOver's keys for headings, tracked changes and
spelling errors (VO Command H, R, E). Tables say each cell with its headings. In Excel, cells are read as you move.
Word's native caret boundaries use Word/macOS's sound; Waypoint keeps its own review and table-edge feedback.

Calendar (experimental)

Read as a diary, in day, week and month view: Left and Right a day at a time, with its number of events; **Up and
Down through that day's events, the time first; Option Up and Down a week at a time; Return** opens an event.
Command T goes to today.

Pages, Numbers and Keynote (experimental)

- Pages: headings by VO Command H, tables cell by cell with VO Shift Down, comments and highlights read in
  place, the page said as you reach it.
- Numbers: an arrow on an empty sheet goes into its first table; moving says each cell with its column's or
  row's heading ("Sales, 1521"); VO Command T and Shift T go between tables.
- Keynote: moving between slides reads the slide, title first, with its notes when they are showing. While a
  presentation plays, each slide is read as you move (macOS asks once whether Waypoint may control Keynote).

Terminal

New output is read as it arrives; your own typing isn't read twice. Waypoint Up and Down review earlier lines.

Steam

Steam's Big Picture is read as you move, once you turn it on: Settings, Other apps' speech, Read Steam's Big Picture, then Restart Steam there. It's
off to begin with because reading it makes Steam open a debugging port on your Mac while Steam runs, which other
programs on the same Mac could use too; turning it off, or uninstalling Waypoint, removes that again. Games that
support it speak through Waypoint without any of this.


KEYBOARD STYLES

Waypoint speaks three screen readers' keys. Press Command Control Comma, then K, until you hear the one you want; the
Waypoint menu, Settings, Keyboard, and the Settings window offer the same choice. The style changes which keys you
press, never what Waypoint can do.

VoiceOver keys (the Mac style)

VoiceOver's own keys, doing what they do in VoiceOver. VO is Control and Option together; Caps Lock works as VO too.
VO arrows move around, VO Space presses, VO Shift Down and Up go into and out of things, VO U is the rotor, VO M the
menu bar, VO D the Dock, VO Shift M the shortcut menu. VO Command Shift with the arrows changes speech settings, VO
Command with the arrows moves by the rotor's kind. Control pauses speech. One Quick Nav is shared with VoiceOver.

Every key, VoiceOver keys.

NVDA keys (the Windows style)

NVDA's own keys, doing what they do in NVDA. Caps Lock is the NVDA key (Control and Option works too): Caps Lock N
the Waypoint menu, Caps Lock 1 keyboard help, Caps Lock L the current line, Caps Lock A say all, Caps Lock F7 the
elements list, Caps Lock Space browse mode on and off. Control stops speech. Where NVDA uses Alt, Waypoint uses the
Mac's Command key, which sits where Alt does on a PC keyboard. The role is said before the words, as NVDA says them.

Every key, NVDA keys.

JAWS keys

JAWS's laptop layout, with Caps Lock as the JAWS key. The reading grid: Caps Lock U, I and O for the previous, current
and next line; J, K and L for words; M, Comma and Period for characters; Y, H and N for sentences; Control U, I and O
for paragraphs. Caps Lock Shift J and L read the line up to and from the cursor. Caps Lock Z is the virtual cursor,
Caps Lock F5, F6 and F7 the lists of form fields, headings and links, Caps Lock Semicolon the PC cursor and Caps Lock P
the JAWS cursor, Caps Lock 8 and 9 click. During say all, Left and Right go back and on a sentence, and Page Up and
Page Down change the speed for that say all. Every other key is NVDA's.

Every key, JAWS keys.

On a Mac keyboard

- Insert doesn't exist, so every Insert, NVDA or JAWS key chord becomes Caps Lock.
- The Mac's own shortcuts win. Where a Windows reader's key collides with a Mac shortcut (Command Comma opens
  every app's settings, Command Period cancels), the reader's command sits on a second key, listed on its page.
- Windows' Control is often the Mac's Command for app shortcuts: copy, find, new tab. In screen reader chords it
  stays Control.
- Home, End, Page Up and Page Down are Fn with the arrows on a laptop keyboard. F1 to F12 need Fn unless the Mac
  is set to use them as standard function keys.

In every style

- Waypoint's own commands behind Command Control Comma never change with the style.
- Keyboard help (the Waypoint menu, Keys) says what any key does without doing it.
- Command Control Comma, then Slash, lists every key that works where you are.
- Your own keys (the Waypoint menu, Keys, My own keys) sit on top of any style.


WAYPOINT AND VOICEOVER

Waypoint is a second screen reader for the Mac, a choice beside VoiceOver. One reader speaks at a time, as on every
other computer: while Waypoint is on, it reads everything and VoiceOver is off; turn Waypoint off and VoiceOver is
back. You choose which you use, and how often you switch.

What to expect

- Command Control Period turns Waypoint off and on, from anywhere. Waypoint turns VoiceOver off as it starts and
  back on as it quits, so you are never without speech.
- Waypoint reads password fields and macOS's own prompts (passwords, Touch ID, "Allow" questions) itself. What you
  type into a password field is never spoken or logged: you hear a click or "star" for each
  character, never the character itself. Privacy and security says more.
- If Waypoint stops or gets stuck for more than a few seconds, VoiceOver comes on, and Waypoint starts again.
- Nothing of VoiceOver's is changed: its settings, voices and Activities stay as they are.

Keeping some apps with VoiceOver

If VoiceOver reads an app better for you, it can keep that app while Waypoint reads the rest. In the app, press
Command Control Shift T: VoiceOver takes it over, and Waypoint remembers your choice next time. The same key gives
it back to Waypoint. The first press turns on Readers per app, in Settings, Screen reader behaviour, where you can
turn it off again.

The list of VoiceOver's apps is under Tools in the Waypoint menu, where you can also remove one. With Readers per app
on you can also choose a mode: Waypoint everywhere except your VoiceOver apps (the usual one), Waypoint only in its
own apps, or VoiceOver everywhere.

The swap

Settings, Screen reader behaviour, Waypoint and VoiceOver swap is on unless you turn it off. On, one reader comes
on as the other goes off. Off, each has its own switch and neither turns the other on or off, which is for VoiceOver
reading inside a virtual machine such as Parallels. Either way, while VoiceOver is on, Waypoint is quiet.

CoExist (experimental)

CoExist is an older way of working, kept as an experiment and off unless you turn it on (Settings, Screen reader
behaviour, Experimental). With it, VoiceOver keeps running beside Waypoint and is silenced at the sound level wherever
Waypoint reads; VoiceOver is heard in the apps you give it, in password fields and in macOS's own prompts. Waypoint's
own braille is off while it's on, as VoiceOver keeps the display. Changing it restarts Waypoint.

It can leave a Mac silent if macOS asks for audio permission while VoiceOver is muted, and a macOS or VoiceOver update
could change how it behaves. Please report anything odd.


TROUBLESHOOTING AND FEEDBACK

Waypoint stops answering

VoiceOver comes back by itself within a few seconds, and your keys work again; if Waypoint is still stuck, it
restarts itself. Command Control Period stops or starts it from anywhere.

No sound at all

If even VoiceOver is silent, macOS's sound service may have stopped. In Terminal, run this and type your password:

    sudo killall coreaudiod


Or restart the Mac. Then press Command Control Period to start Waypoint.

Force quit, spoken

Command Option Escape lists the running apps: choose one, then force quit it, ask it to quit, or cancel.

Sending feedback

- The moment something goes wrong: Command Control Comma, J marks it, and you carry on.
- When you're ready: the Waypoint menu, Send feedback. A report is saved to your Desktop, and Waypoint's problem form
  opens on GitHub with your build and Mac filled in. Say in a sentence what you were doing and what you expected, and
  drag the report in if you like. Reports on GitHub are public: choose the report without what Waypoint said if
  anything private was on screen.
- To help with an app: switch to it, then the Waypoint menu, This app, Help improve this app. Tell us what's wrong
  opens the form on GitHub, filled in with how the app is built (never what's in it). There's more in
  Helping Waypoint read your apps.
- You need a free GitHub account to post. Waypoint is a spare-time project: every report is read, but replies may
  take a while.

Removing Waypoint

The Waypoint menu, About and updates, Uninstall Waypoint: the app only (your settings kept), or everything
(settings, logs, permissions and login items), which also turns VoiceOver on and removes anything an earlier Waypoint put into VoiceOver's settings.


PRIVACY AND SECURITY

A screen reader has to see everything on your Mac and hear every key, so it asks for macOS's most powerful
permissions. This page says what each is for, what never leaves your Mac, and how you can check for yourself.

The permissions, and why

- Accessibility: to read apps, windows and controls, and to move and press them for you. Every screen reader
  needs it.
- Input Monitoring: to hear Waypoint's keys (VO keys, the Waypoint layer, quick navigation). Waypoint looks at a
  key to see whether it is one of its commands; anything else goes straight on to the app.
- Screen Recording: only for screen recognition and describing a picture, when you ask. Both run on your Mac.
- Automation (asked once, for one app at a time): for example Keynote, to read a slide while a show plays.
- Audio (CoExist, experimental and off unless you turn it on): to keep VoiceOver's sound quiet while Waypoint reads. A small separate program,
  voiceover-quiet, mutes VoiceOver's sound and throws it away: nothing is recorded, saved or sent, and no other
  app's sound is touched.

Waypoint does not ask for Full Disk Access.

What leaves your Mac

Waypoint connects to these places by itself:

- Its update site, `doll-eye.github.io/turnover-updates`, to check for a new build. Nothing about you or what you
  do is sent: it only fetches that file.
- Apple's public catalog search, `itunes.apple.com`, when you search in the App Store, Music or Podcasts. The
  words you searched for and your Mac's region go to Apple, as the app sends them to Apple anyway; Waypoint reads the
  answers as a plain list.
- Hugging Face, `huggingface.co`, once, the first time you choose a natural voice: Waypoint's voice helper
  downloads the voice, about 170 MB, and keeps it on your Mac. Nothing you read or type is sent; after that the
  natural voices need no connection.

Everything else happens on your Mac:

- Speech, including the natural voices, is made on your Mac.
- Screen recognition and picture descriptions use Apple's on-device recognition and model.
- Game speech listens only on this Mac (it accepts nothing from the network), and never speaks over Apple's apps or a
  browser. Reading Steam's Big Picture is off until you turn it on, because it makes Steam open a debugging port on
  this Mac while it runs; turning it off, or uninstalling Waypoint, removes that again.

Things you choose to send, and see before they go:

- Send feedback saves a report on your Desktop and opens Waypoint's problem form on GitHub in your browser. You
  write the report yourself, and attach the file only if you choose to; Waypoint never sends anything on its own.
  Reports on GitHub are public.
- Ask for this app to be tuned opens the request form on GitHub, filled in with how the app is built (kinds of
  item and counts, never what is in it), for you to post.

Check it yourself

You don't have to take our word for it. Install a firewall that shows every connection, such as LuLu (free, from
objective-see.org) or Little Snitch, and use Waypoint as usual. You will see Waypoint contact TurnOver's update site, and
nothing else.

What Waypoint keeps

Waypoint keeps a log on your Mac, in your Library folder: Application Support, TurnOver, logs. Logs older than 30
days are deleted.

- The log records what Waypoint did: the app, the kind of item, the command a key ran.
- It never records what you type. A typed key is written as "a typed key", never the key itself.
- It doesn't record the words you were reading. Where a name, a page or a message would appear, the log keeps only
  how many characters it had.

The log is plain text: you are welcome to open it and read it.

A feedback report holds these logs. "Send a report with what Waypoint said" also includes what Waypoint said aloud in
the last 15 minutes, which can be the text of an email or page you were reading; "Send a report without what
Waypoint said" leaves it out. Either way the report is a file on your Desktop: you decide whether to attach it, and
since GitHub reports are public, choose the one without what Waypoint said if anything private was on screen.

Who can talk to Waypoint

Waypoint's own helpers (its Settings window, the Command Control Period helper, the plugin tool, Windy) reach it
through a private channel in Waypoint's own folder. Before anything is read, macOS checks that the program on the other
end is signed by TurnOver's developer; any other program is turned away and the refusal is logged. A copy you build
yourself without a developer certificate accepts only the programs inside its own app. Like any app's, Waypoint's
settings file can be changed by programs running as you; a script you give a key to asks you before it first runs.

Apps that speak through Waypoint

An app can ask Waypoint to speak for it (a game, for example), through a port on this Mac only. Waypoint works out
for itself which program is asking (from the Mac's own records, not from what the app says), and the first time asks
you, by name, whether you trust it. An app you allow is heard only while it is the app in front, and it can do nothing
but speak: no settings, no keys. Settings, Apps that speak through Waypoint, lists them and changes your answer.

Passwords and security prompts

Waypoint reads password fields and macOS's own prompts itself, and keeps what you type private:

- Password fields, in any app or web page: Waypoint says the field, and as you type you hear a click or "star" for each
  character, never the character. While a password field has the keyboard,
  macOS keeps your keys from every app but the one you are typing in, Waypoint included.
- Typing into a password field is never spoken or logged by Waypoint, even where an app hides that the keyboard
  is in one.
- macOS's own prompts (an administrator password, the keychain, Touch ID, "Allow" questions): Waypoint reads the
  prompt when it appears and puts you in it. macOS doesn't let another program press buttons in some of these, so if
  Waypoint's press is refused, it asks you to press Space yourself.

If you'd rather VoiceOver had these, CoExist (experimental, in Settings, Screen reader behaviour) gives password fields
and macOS's prompts to VoiceOver. See Waypoint and VoiceOver.

When we get something wrong

We'll say so here and in What's new.

- 18 October 2026: in Kindle's Amazon sign-in, Waypoint read the password aloud as it was typed, because the page
  told Waypoint the keyboard was somewhere other than the password field. It was found the same day and fixed for
  every app: typing into a password field is never spoken or logged, wherever the app says the keyboard is. It
  needed an app that hides where the keyboard is, as Kindle's sign-in does; Kindle support was new that day, so we
  know of no released build where it happened.

Questions

Ask on TurnOver's GitHub page (github.com/TurnOver-For-Mac/turnover), from the Waypoint menu, Send feedback. Something that
could expose what a person reads or types is best reported privately, through the repository's Security tab.


VOICEOVER KEYS: EVERY KEY

This page is made from Waypoint's own command tables (`turnover-coretest --keys-reference`, 14 Nov 2026), so it matches the build it came with. Don't edit it by hand.

The VoiceOver keys style (the Mac style): VoiceOver's own keys, doing what they do in VoiceOver. Waypoint in a key means the Waypoint key: Control and Option together (VO), or Caps Lock. Keys VoiceOver has that aren't listed here go to VoiceOver as usual where VoiceOver reads, and do nothing special where Waypoint reads. Keyboard help (the Waypoint menu, Keys) says what any key does without doing it; Command Control Comma, then Slash, lists the keys that work where you are.

Switch styles with Command Control Comma, then K. Waypoint's own commands behind Command Control Comma are the same in every style: see The Waypoint layer.

Waypoint

  Key: What it does
  Control Command 0: Describes the app in front: its window and the parts of it; pressed twice, as a list to move to a part
  Control Command W: Says what is new in this part of the window since you left it; pressed twice, in every part of the app
  Option Command Escape: Force quit an application; lists the running applications to force quit one, with a confirmation (the Mac's Force Quit window, spoken)
  Waypoint Command A: Anywhere: type to find an app, a menu command, an action or a Waypoint command, and run it
  Waypoint Command Equals: Marks this moment in the log, for a report that something just sounded wrong (VO-Command-Equals)
  Waypoint Option B: Bookmarks the item you are on, so a key can bring you back to it
  Waypoint Option G: Lists your bookmarks, narrowed as you type; Return goes to one, in its app
  Waypoint Option I: After reading around a text field, puts the reviewed text into the field as it is
  Waypoint Option N: In a list of messages, files or results, switches Up and Down to skimming the items by their key words, quickly, and back; Return reads the item in full
  Waypoint Option P: Lists the places you stayed at recently, the one before this first; Return goes back to it
  Waypoint Option Q: After reading around a text field, puts the reviewed text into the field as a quote
  Waypoint Option R: Review layer: once, the next key reads around the field instead of typing; twice, the layer stays on until pressed again
  Waypoint Option S: After reading around a text field, says where the review cursor is
  Waypoint Option Y: After reading around a text field, copies the reviewed text
  Waypoint Option Command Shift Slash: Puts the second reader back with the keyboard: it follows the focus again, in this app and window (it stays where an area jump put it until then)

VoiceOver keys

  Key: What it does

NVDA KEYS: EVERY KEY

This page is made from Waypoint's own command tables (`turnover-coretest --keys-reference`, 14 Nov 2026), so it matches the build it came with. Don't edit it by hand.

The NVDA keys style (the Windows style): NVDA's own keys, doing what they do in NVDA. Waypoint in a key means the NVDA key: Caps Lock, or Control and Option together. NVDA's Alt is the Mac's Command key, by position. Keyboard help (the Waypoint menu, Keys) says what any key does without doing it; Command Control Comma, then Slash, lists the keys that work where you are.

Switch styles with Command Control Comma, then K. Waypoint's own commands behind Command Control Comma are the same in every style: see The Waypoint layer.

Waypoint

  Key: What it does
  Control Command 0: Describes the app in front: its window and the parts of it; pressed twice, as a list to move to a part
  Control Command W: Says what is new in this part of the window since you left it; pressed twice, in every part of the app
  Option Command Escape: Force quit an application; lists the running applications to force quit one, with a confirmation (the Mac's Force Quit window, spoken)
  Waypoint Command A: Anywhere: type to find an app, a menu command, an action or a Waypoint command, and run it
  Waypoint Command Equals: Marks this moment in the log, for a report that something just sounded wrong (VO-Command-Equals)
  Waypoint Option B: Bookmarks the item you are on, so a key can bring you back to it
  Waypoint Option G: Lists your bookmarks, narrowed as you type; Return goes to one, in its app
  Waypoint Option I: After reading around a text field, puts the reviewed text into the field as it is
  Waypoint Option N: In a list of messages, files or results, switches Up and Down to skimming the items by their key words, quickly, and back; Return reads the item in full
  Waypoint Option P: Lists the places you stayed at recently, the one before this first; Return goes back to it
  Waypoint Option Q: After reading around a text field, puts the reviewed text into the field as a quote
  Waypoint Option R: Review layer: once, the next key reads around the field instead of typing; twice, the layer stays on until pressed again
  Waypoint Option S: After reading around a text field, says where the review cursor is
  Waypoint Option Y: After reading around a text field, copies the reviewed text
  Waypoint Option Command Shift Slash: Puts the second reader back with the keyboard: it follows the focus again, in this app and window (it stays where an area jump put it until then)

JAWS KEYS: EVERY KEY

This page is made from Waypoint's own command tables (`turnover-coretest --keys-reference`, 14 Nov 2026), so it matches the build it came with. Don't edit it by hand.

The JAWS keys style: JAWS's laptop layout. Waypoint in a key means the JAWS key: Caps Lock, or Control and Option together. Only the keys that differ from NVDA's are JAWS's own; every other key is NVDA's, so the NVDA keys page lists the rest. Keyboard help (the Waypoint menu, Keys) says what any key does without doing it; Command Control Comma, then Slash, lists the keys that work where you are.

Switch styles with Command Control Comma, then K. Waypoint's own commands behind Command Control Comma are the same in every style: see The Waypoint layer.

PLUGINS

A plugin teaches Waypoint an app: what to call its controls, which keys do what, what to read when something
changes, and what to keep quiet. Plugins are written as data, not code: a folder with a manifest and a few JSON
files. That means no programming, nothing a plugin can do behind your back, and anyone can read one before
installing it.

Using one

- Fixes that come with Waypoint: ones people shared and the project accepted. They're simply there, and listed in
  the plugins menu as "comes with Waypoint".
- The plugin library (the Waypoint menu, Tools, Plugins, Plugin library) is paused for now.
- Install from a folder or the clipboard, in the same menu, for one someone sent you.
- Reload plugins after editing one.

Making one

1. Switch to the app, then the Waypoint menu, Tools, Plugins.
2. Choose Write a plugin for this app yourself: Waypoint makes a starter folder with the guide, a snapshot of the
   app's window and its menus, so you can see what the app calls things.
3. Or choose Make a plugin with an AI: Waypoint gives it the window's labels, text and menus and its own log, with
   your typing and speech left out, and the AI writes the first draft. You read and change it.
4. Edit the files, then Install my plugin from its folder. Reload after each change.

The format is in the plugin guide: what a
manifest holds, how a rule names a control, how a key is given a command, and the checker that finds mistakes.

Sharing one

The quickest way is a fix made in Waypoint itself (VO-Slash with VoiceOver keys, or This app, Change the item I'm on):
choose Share my changes on GitHub in its list. Waypoint copies the fix and opens a short form. Fixes the project
accepts come with Waypoint for everyone. Helping Waypoint read your apps has the steps.

The plugin library is paused for now (since October 2026): shared fixes reach people with Waypoint itself instead.

What a plugin can't do

Run code, reach the internet, read anything in an app it doesn't name, or change Waypoint's settings. Those limits
are the point: a screen reader sees everything on your screen, and a plugin shouldn't widen that.


HELPING WAYPOINT READ YOUR APPS

You know better than anyone how an app should sound. There are a few ways to help Waypoint read one better, from a
minute's work to building a fix with your own AI assistant. Start wherever suits you; each one helps.

With the app in front, open the Waypoint menu (Command Control Comma, then M), then This app, then
Help improve this app.

Tell us what's wrong

Choose Tell us what's wrong. A short form opens in your browser, already filled in with the app, your Waypoint
build and how the app is built: the kinds of item it has, never what's in them. You add what you did and what you
heard, then submit it. Nothing to install; you need a free GitHub account.

Fix the item you're on

On a button or a line that reads badly:

- With VoiceOver keys, press VO-Slash.
- With any keys, choose Fix the item I'm on in Help improve this app, or This app, then Change the item I'm on.

A short list opens:

- Label this item: type a name, and it's called that from now on.
- Give this item a key: press a key, and it presses, focuses or reads the item.
- Read this when it changes: for a status line or a counter that changes silently.
- Don't announce this: for something that chatters.
- Undo the last change, and My changes for this app, to take one away.

Each change works straight away, and is kept on your Mac for that app.

Share your fix

In the same list, choose Share my changes on GitHub. Waypoint copies your changes and opens a short form. Paste
them where it asks, read them through to check they hold nothing private, and submit. If the project accepts them,
they come with the next Waypoint, for everyone who uses that app.

Save my changes as a file puts them in a zip on your Desktop instead, to send to someone.

Build a fix with your AI

Some apps need more than a label or a key: what to read after Return, or how to move through a list that doesn't
report anything. That's an app's module, a little code for that one app. Your own AI assistant can write it with
you, one step at a time, while you decide how it should sound.

Choose Build it with Claude Code (or Codex, or Gemini CLI, if you have them). Your assistant opens with a
description of the app and Waypoint's brief for AI contributors, asks you what goes wrong, and checks with you
before anything is sent. You need Xcode, Apple's free tool for building apps (a large download), and a free GitHub
account; your assistant helps you set them up.

While you try your fix, it installs as Waypoint Developer, beside Waypoint. The Waypoint menu then offers
Switch to your developer build and Switch to the published Waypoint, so you can go back and forth.
Command Control Period starts and stops whichever you used last.

Build it myself opens the same description of the app, and the guide to writing a module.

Ask for a building block

Now and then an app needs something new from Waypoint itself. Choose Ask for a building block: a short form
opens, already filled in. Say what the app needs; you don't need to know how to build it.

What stays safe

Fixes are data, not code: Waypoint checks every one as it loads it, and a fix can't reach the internet, read another
app, or change your settings. Modules are reviewed by the project before they're accepted, and nothing in Waypoint's
core changes without that review. Nothing you share includes what you read or type unless you paste it yourself, so
read what you send.

Thank you.


THE LAUNCHER

The launcher runs an action from a key you hold: open an app, bring it forward, run a shell command, open a web page or
a file, or run any Waypoint command. It is on by default.

How it works

- Hold Right Command and press a letter: that letter's action runs, and the key is not typed or passed to the app.
  This is layer 1.
- Hold Right Command and Right Option and press a key: layer 2, a separate table that starts empty. It never
  also runs layer 1's key.
- A key with no binding, and Right Command held with any other modifier (Shift, Control, Left Option, Left Command),
  goes through to the app untouched. Right Command alone behaves as it always did.
- When Waypoint has handed over to VoiceOver, the launcher does nothing.

Opening an app: it launches if it isn't running, comes forward if it is, and if it is already in front, the next window
comes forward. Waypoint says "Opening Google Chrome" or just the app's name. An app that isn't installed is announced
as "not installed".

The keys that come with it (layer 1)

G Google Chrome, H GitHub Copilot, O Microsoft Outlook, M Microsoft Teams, S Safari, F Finder, I Messages,
X Microsoft Excel, Z Terminal, C Colour Contrast Analyser, T TextEdit.

Equals raises the volume and Minus lowers it, one step a press, and repeating after a second's hold (see
Volume below).

Volume

The commands volumeUp and volumeDown change the volume of the current sound output device, the way the keyboard's
volume keys do, by one step: one percent unless you choose another. Each press reads the volume the device has at that
moment, so changes made elsewhere are respected; raising it from mute switches the mute off. Nothing is spoken on a
normal press. If there is no sound device, or it has no volume that can be set, Waypoint says so (at most once a
second). volumeSetStep lists the step sizes, half a percent to ten; the Launcher pane has the same choice, and the
setting is `waypoint.volume.step`.

Speech that is playing carries on when a volume key is pressed or held, as with VoiceOver's volume keys. That is a
launcher setting of the binding, `keepSpeech` (below), on for the two shipped volume keys; every other key still stops
speech.

After each change Waypoint plays the volume sound macOS plays for its own volume keys (at the new volume, on the
current output device, without stopping or lowering speech), at most about ten times a second while a key is held. It
is not played when the volume was already at its top or bottom, and it is played when raising the volume from mute. It
follows the system setting Play feedback when volume is changed (Sound settings; on if never set) and Waypoint's own
switch: the command volumeFeedbackEnable toggles it, there is a checkbox in the Launcher pane, and the setting is
`waypoint.volume.feedback`.

These take over from the usual shortcuts. Right Command with C, X, Z, S, F, T, H or M used to be Copy, Cut, Undo,
Save, Find, New Tab, Hide or Minimise. Left Command still does all of those. Delete or change a binding to get a
shortcut back.

Changing it

- Settings, Launcher: a list per layer with Add, Edit and Delete, a key field, the held keys, and Reset to
  defaults. Everything in it works from the keyboard and with a screen reader.
- The file: `~/Library/Application Support/TurnOver/launcher.json`. It is plain JSON you can edit by hand. Waypoint
  picks up a change by itself; the command launcherReload reads it at once.
- Held keys: the commands launcherSetHold1 and launcherSetHold2 wait for you to press the keys you want
  held, launcherResetKeys puts back Right Command and Right Command with Right Option, and launcherEnable
  switches the launcher on or off. launcherList lists the bindings; choose one to run it.

The file

```json
{
  "version": 1,
  "layer1": [
    {"key": "g", "kind": "app", "name": "Google Chrome", "bundle": "com.google.Chrome"},
    {"key": "u", "kind": "open", "name": "Docs", "target": "https://example.com"},
    {"key": "d", "kind": "shell", "name": "Backup", "target": "~/bin/backup.sh", "timeout": 60},
    {"key": "l", "kind": "command", "target": "launcherList"}
  ],
  "layer2": []
}
```

- `kind` is `app`, `shell`, `open` or `command`.
- app: `bundle` (the identifier) and/or `name`. Either is enough; the name is looked for in the running apps and the
  Applications folders.
- shell: `target` is run with `/bin/zsh -c` in your home folder, in the background, and stopped after `timeout`
  seconds (30 if missing, 300 at most). Its output is thrown away. If it fails, Waypoint says so.
- open: `target` is a web address or a file or folder path (a leading `~` is your home folder).
- command: `target` is the name of a Waypoint command, as in the key lists.
- Hold to repeat (any kind): `repeatRate` is how many times a second to run it while the key stays held, 1 to 30;
  `repeatDelay` is how many seconds to hold before it starts, 0.2 to 5 (1 if missing). The action runs once as the key
  goes down, then repeats until you let go of the key or Right Command, switch the launcher off, or Waypoint hands
  over. Without `repeatRate` the action runs once. Example:
  `{"key": "=", "kind": "command", "target": "volumeUp", "repeatDelay": 1, "repeatRate": 15}`.
- Keep speech (any kind): `"keepSpeech": true` leaves speech that is playing alone when the key is pressed or held;
  without it (the usual) the key stops speech like any other. Example:
  `{"key": "=", "kind": "command", "target": "volumeUp", "repeatRate": 15, "keepSpeech": true}`.
- `key` is a letter, a digit, or a key name from the key lists, such as `upArrow` or `f5`.
- Unknown fields are kept out of the way, and an entry that can't work is skipped and logged; the rest load.

A file that can't be read at all is logged and copied next to itself as `launcher.json.corrupt-` and the date; the
built-in keys are used and your file is left alone. Whenever Waypoint saves, the file it replaces is kept as
`launcher.json.bak`.

Limits

- Cycling windows brings the back window of the app forward. For an app that lists its windows in a fixed order, two
  windows may swap places rather than rotate.
- Repeating a held key does not run most actions again: only a binding with `repeatRate` repeats (see above). The
  system's own key repeat is swallowed with the key.
- A sound device whose volume moves in coarse hardware steps may round a step smaller than its own.


READING TOOLS

These Waypoint features help you move through busy apps quickly. Each can be switched off, and every key can be changed in
the key list. "VO" below means the Waypoint key (Caps Lock or whichever you chose).

Skim: hear a list quickly

Use it in a list of messages, files or results.

1. Press VO-Option-N. Waypoint says "skim on" and how many items there are.
2. Up and Down now say each item's key words only, fast, with a tick between items. The speech rate returns to
   normal a moment after the last item.
3. Return says the item in full and leaves the cursor on it, and skimming ends.
4. Escape or VO-Option-N again ends it without choosing.

Skim ends by itself when the focus moves to something else, you switch apps, a menu opens or you leave it alone for five
minutes. Nothing plays by itself. If it says "skim is off", run the skimEnable command.

Review layer: read around while you type

In a text field, such as a chat box, you can read the page or window without leaving the field and without typing
anything. The layer is on by default.

1. Hold Left Option + Right Shift and press a key. Each press is one step and the field keeps the keyboard.
   Or tap VO-Option-R, then press the key; tap it twice to keep the layer on until you press it again. Escape ends
   it.
2. Keys: letters are browse-mode quick keys (H next heading, with Left Shift added, previous); Up and Down
   read by line, Left and Right by character, Right Option with them by word; Home, End and the
   Page keys jump; Tab goes to the next link or control (Left Shift for the previous), in the review only,
   and never moves the real focus; Return presses what is under the cursor. Quick keys do not wrap round the page.
   From a chat's message box the review starts at the latest message. It keeps its place while you type; it moves to a
   message only when a new one arrives, and says "new message".
3. Left Shift with the chord and an arrow selects text in the field (with Right Option, by word).
4. Afterwards, for five minutes: VO-Option-S says where the review cursor is, VO-Option-Q puts the reviewed text
   into the field as a quote, VO-Option-I inserts it as it is, and VO-Option-Y copies it.

Passwords and read-only fields are refused. VO-Shift-F5 and a click with the Waypoint key held (no click reaches the
app) put the review position where the mouse pointer is.

Places: bookmarks and recent places

- VO-Option-B bookmarks the current item, named from its text.
- VO-Option-G opens your bookmarks: type part of a name and press Return to go there.
- VO-Option-P lists recent places, newest first.

Bookmarks are saved in `places.json` in Waypoint's support folder. The command placesDelete removes one.

Focus memory: return to where you were

- Switching away from an app and back puts the focus where you left it, if the app has not already done so itself.
- In apps with named regions (Teams, GitHub Copilot, Notes), moving to another region and back lands on the item you were
  on and says so. In a message region it lands on the newest message if one has arrived, and says "newest".
- It is kept across restarts for a week, as short fingerprints (roles and up to 80 characters of text), in
  `focus-memory.json`. focusMemoryPersist turns that off and focusMemoryForget empties it.

Areas: jump to a part of any window

- Right Option 1 to 9 go to the window's panes and bands, in layout order; the side panel is last.
- Right Option 0 lists the app's bars and every toolbar button. On a tool area (a ribbon, a formatter) the digit opens
  its controls as a list; on a list, table or page it puts the focus there.
- VO-Option-O lists the controls of the area you are in (twice, the global ones). VO-Option-M goes to the main
  content, VO-Option-A to the first list, VO-Option-F to the search field.
- With the second reader (above), hold its chord as well to jump the second reader instead of the keyboard.

The details are in `docs/AREAS.md`.

Anywhere, actions, what changed, describe

- VO-Command-A opens Anywhere: type to narrow a list of apps, the front app's menu commands, what can be done to the
  item you are on, the app's parts and Waypoint's own commands. Return runs one, Escape closes it.
- VO-Command-Space lists everything that can be done to the item you are on, including its right-click menu.
- Control-Command-W says what is new in this part of the window since you left it; twice, in every part of the app.
- Control-Command-0 describes the app in front and its parts; twice, as a list where Return goes to a part.
- In a list, a letter jumps to the next item that starts with it, whatever the sort order; letters typed together make a
  prefix.

Sounds

Only skim plays a sound when it starts and ends, and a tick between items. The other tools do not mark themselves with a sound.

Changing the keys

Every key above is a command in the key list, category Waypoint. Choose a command and press a new key, or remove its key.
The Right Command launcher is described in the launcher.
